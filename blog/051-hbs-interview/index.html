The HBS Interview — 30 Questions in 30 Minutes, and What That Means for You

Most MBA interviews involve 10-15 questions over 30 minutes. Harvard Business School asks 30-40.

That's not a typo. HBS moves fast — and understanding why changes how you prepare.

What Makes HBS Different

The HBS interview is non-blind and comprehensive. The admissions committee member sitting across from you has already read your entire application — your essays, résumé, and recommendations — before the conversation begins.

At most MBA programmes, alumni conduct interviews without access to your application. At HBS, an admissions committee member does it, and they've done their reading.

This changes the dynamic significantly. They're not trying to understand your background for the first time. They already have a picture of you. The interview is about testing the coherence of that picture under real-time pressure — and pushing on the parts that feel unclear.

The Speed: 30-40 Questions in 30 Minutes

The pace is deliberate. With that many questions in 30 minutes, you can't over-explain any single answer. HBS is forcing you to be concise, prioritise what matters, and communicate clearly under time pressure.

The interviewer will move on even if you're still mid-thought. If they say "let's move on," they mean it.

Preparation for this isn't just knowing your answers — it's knowing them briefly. Practice delivering your key career moments in 30-45 seconds, not 2-3 minutes.

What HBS Is Actually Evaluating

A former HBS admissions committee member describes it this way: "The content of your answer matters less than the coherence of your decision-making logic across different parts of your life."

They're not scoring individual answers. They're looking for a consistent thread connecting your decisions — why you chose your college, your field, your company, your pivot points. Every major choice in your life should have a reason, and those reasons should be consistent.

Weak candidates have inconsistencies between what they wrote in their essays and what they say in the interview. Strong candidates tell the same story — in both, the motivation is the same, even if the language changes.

The "Why" Behind Everything

HBS interviewers ask "why?" repeatedly. Not just "why HBS?" but:

  • Why did you go to that university?
  • Why did you choose your first employer?
  • Why did you leave?
  • Why this next move?
  • Why now?

Prepare for follow-up. The first answer is rarely the final answer at HBS. They drill.

The Post-Interview Reflection

HBS is the only MBA programme with a formal post-interview reflection. Within 24 hours of your interview, you submit a written reflection via the HBS admissions system.

It is not a prepared statement. It should include:

  • A brief thank-you to your interviewer
  • An honest reflection on how the interview went
  • Specific points from the conversation you found meaningful
  • What you took away and what it clarified for you

The key word is "authentic." HBS knows what prepared statements look like. The reflection is meant to show self-awareness, not polish.

Keep it under one page.

"Why HBS?" — The Question That Separates Candidates

Generic answers fail here more visibly than almost anywhere. "The case method," "the network," and "the calibre of classmates" are insufficient — every candidate says some version of this.

Strong "Why HBS?" answers reference specific professors, courses, clubs, or initiatives. They explain why HBS in particular is the right environment for where the candidate is going — not just that it's a great school.

If another school's name could be substituted into your answer without changing it, the answer isn't finished.

Selection Reality

Around 20% of HBS applicants are invited to interview. Of those interviewed, roughly 60% receive offers.

If you've reached the interview stage, you're already a credible candidate. The interview is the final test of fit — and coherence is what passes it.
