Virtual Interview Setup: Camera, Audio, Lighting and Background

Virtual Interview Setup: Camera, Audio, Lighting and Background

A technical problem in a video interview creates an impression that has nothing to do with your qualifications.

You can recover from a slow internet connection if you acknowledge it and move on. You cannot easily recover from the hiring manager's first impression being that you look like you're in a dimly-lit cupboard — even if everything you say afterwards is excellent.

A functional virtual interview setup takes about 20 minutes to arrange and test. Here's what to check.

Camera

Position: The camera should be at eye level or very slightly above. A laptop camera on a desk puts the camera below your eyeline, which creates an unflattering angle and makes you appear to be looking down. Raise the laptop on a stand, a stack of books, or a box until the camera is level with your face.

Distance: Aim for your face and shoulders to fill roughly the top two-thirds of the frame. You shouldn't be a tiny figure in the distance or a close-up of just your face.

Eye contact: When speaking, look at the camera — not at the person on your screen. Looking at their face on screen looks like you're looking slightly down from their perspective. It's a small thing, but in a 30-minute call it's consistently noticeable.

Audio

External microphone or headphones: A pair of earbuds with a built-in mic produces notably better audio than a laptop's built-in microphone in most home environments. The difference matters — muffled or echo-heavy audio makes it harder for the interviewer to follow you and creates cognitive load for both of you.

Environment: Find the quietest room available. Close the door. Mute notifications on your phone and computer. Silence anything that might interrupt (dishwashers, washing machines, fans, air conditioning). Tell anyone in the house you're on an important call.

Test your audio before the day: Call a friend, or record yourself for 30 seconds in the same room, and listen back. Most people don't know what they actually sound like on a call until they hear it.

Lighting

Light source in front of you, not behind: If your main light source is a window behind you, your face will be in shadow. Move so the window is in front of you (to the side is also acceptable) — natural light from the front is the best lighting available for most video calls.

Avoid overhead lighting only: A single overhead light creates unflattering shadows under your eyes and chin. A lamp at face level in front of you solves this cheaply and immediately.

Check the whole frame: Sit in your chair, open your camera application, and look at how the lighting falls on your face. If you can see any bright light source directly behind your head in the frame, the interviewer is going to struggle to see your face clearly.

Background

What works: A plain wall, a tidy bookcase, a neutral room. Clean and uncluttered reads as professional.

What to avoid: Busy patterns that compete with your face for attention; unmade beds; kitchens with visible mess; anything distracting or personal that you'd rather not discuss.

Virtual backgrounds: These work adequately in good lighting conditions but often blur or flicker around the edges of your hair and shoulders. A clean real background is more reliable for a formal interview.

The pre-interview check

Run this 10 minutes before the interview starts:

  • Camera positioned at eye level ✓
  • Face well-lit, no bright light source directly behind ✓
  • Headphones or earbuds connected ✓
  • Environment quiet ✓
  • Background tidy ✓
  • Internet connection stable (close anything using bandwidth) ✓
  • Interview platform open and working ✓

If you're practising with easedit.co before the actual interview, set up in the same room and same configuration — you want the session to mimic real conditions, including the camera angle and audio setup you'll use on the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an external camera for a video interview?

No — a modern laptop webcam is sufficient for most interviews. What matters more than hardware is lighting and positioning. A well-lit face with a built-in camera looks significantly better than a poorly-lit face with an expensive camera.

What's the best background for a video interview?

A plain wall or a tidy, uncluttered bookcase works well. Virtual backgrounds are acceptable but can blur unexpectedly — a real background you can control is more reliable. Avoid busy patterns, strong backlighting (windows directly behind you), and anything potentially distracting.

Should I use headphones or the built-in microphone?

Headphones with a built-in mic (earbuds are fine) produce significantly better audio quality than a laptop microphone in most environments. They also prevent echo from your speakers feeding back into the call. Use them.
