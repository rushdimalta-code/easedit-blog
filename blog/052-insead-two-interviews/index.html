INSEAD's Two Alumni Interviews — What to Expect and How to Prepare

Before you speak to a single person at INSEAD, you'll have recorded four video answers and one written response for a platform called Kira Talent. After that, you'll have two separate 45-60 minute interviews, each conducted by a different alumni member, each with full access to your application.

No other top MBA programme has this structure. Understanding why it exists changes how you prepare.

Why Two Interviews?

INSEAD's intake is deeply international — typically 90+ nationalities per cohort. The admissions committee isn't just evaluating your leadership record or academic capacity. They're specifically assessing whether you can function effectively across cultures, adapt to different contexts, and contribute to an environment where cultural diversity is a daily reality, not a talking point.

Two independent alumni interviews create two independent data points about the same candidate. If both interviewers form a similar picture, the committee has confidence. If they diverge significantly, that's useful information too.

The Kira Talent Component

Before the alumni interviews, INSEAD requires a Kira Talent submission: 4 video questions and 1 typed answer. This is time-limited — you typically have one attempt, with a short preparation window before each question.

It's not assessed for polish. It's assessed for authentic communication, clarity of motivation, and writing ability (the typed answer). The questions tend to cover similar ground to the alumni interviews: goals, cultural experiences, why INSEAD.

Practise speaking clearly and concisely on camera before you record. The format is unforgiving if you're not comfortable with it — unlike a live interview, you can't course-correct mid-answer.

What the Alumni Interviewers Are Evaluating

INSEAD publishes four explicit criteria:

1. Leadership and Impact. Not just "I led a team" — evidence that your leadership changed something. Outcomes, not activities.

2. International Motivation and Cultural Fluency. This is the most distinctive INSEAD emphasis. They're looking for specific examples of cultural adaptation — not just "I've worked internationally," but a situation where cultural context required you to think and act differently.

3. Academic Capacity. Can you handle the programme's rigour? Relevant background, intellectual engagement, ability to handle quantitative and analytical demands.

4. Community Contributions. What will you bring to the cohort? INSEAD learning is peer-intensive. They want someone who adds to the room, not just someone who benefits from it.

Preparing for International Motivation

Most candidates underdeliver on this criterion. They describe travelling, working with international teams, or studying abroad — but don't go deeper.

What INSEAD wants is a specific example of cultural adaptation: a situation where you recognised a cultural difference, understood its implications, and adjusted your approach accordingly. The more specific the situation, the more credible the answer.

"I've worked with colleagues across 15 countries" is a fact. "I realised in a client meeting in Japan that my direct style was creating friction, and I changed how I structured feedback for that team for the rest of the engagement" is a story.

The Format and Practical Details

Two separate interviews, each 45-60 minutes. Alumni in your country of residence are typically assigned. The atmosphere is "conversation over interrogation" — INSEAD alumni interviewers are trained to let personality emerge naturally.

Research each interviewer's LinkedIn before the interview. Develop questions specific to their background and their time at INSEAD. This isn't performative — the interviewers know when questions are generic, and it's a missed opportunity to have a genuine conversation with someone who has been through the programme.

A thank-you email after each interview is expected.

What Undermines Candidates

Generic international experience. "I've worked across cultures" without a specific story doesn't land.

Treating it like a corporate interview. Formal, rehearsed, achievement-focused answers read as a lack of self-awareness in a "conversation over interrogation" format.

Vague "Why INSEAD?" INSEAD's international emphasis, Fontainebleau/Singapore campuses, January and September intakes, and alumni network are specific. Answers that could apply to any top MBA don't work here.
